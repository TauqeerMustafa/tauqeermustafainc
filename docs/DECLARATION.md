# Engagement Declaration

**Tauqeer Mustafa Inc.** — Terms & Conditions of Participation

One document for every way somebody joins us: **internship**, **unpaid trial**,
**salaried employment**, or **fixed-scope contract**. The engagement type is
selected in the Engagement Record below, and the Schedule of Application says
which sections bind that type. Sections 5, 6, 9, 10, 12, 13 and 26 bind
**everyone, always**.

Sections 1–10 keep the numbering of the signed *Trial Engagement Declaration*
(v1, 22 June 2026) clause for clause, so the trial task set in
[`frontend/constants/playbooks.ts`](../frontend/constants/playbooks.ts) and the
lead-generation playbook can go on citing 3.4, 3.5, 3.6, 4.1, 5.3, 6.5 and 9.2
and mean the same thing. Sections 11–26 are new. **Do not renumber 1–10.**

---

## Before you use this

Three things the person issuing this needs to know.

1. **This is a template drafted in-house, not legal advice.** Have a Pakistani
   employment lawyer read it once before it goes to a signatory. One review
   covers every future signing.
2. **The post-engagement restraint in §7 is the weak clause.** Section 27 of the
   Contract Act 1872 — in force in Pakistan — voids any agreement that restrains
   a person from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business, and Pakistani
   courts have generally refused to enforce post-employment non-compete
   covenants. What survives is confidentiality (§5), intellectual property (§6),
   and restraint *during* the engagement. §7 is therefore written to lean on
   misuse of confidential information rather than on a bare 12-month ban, and
   §10.6 severs it if a court strikes it. Treat §7 as a deterrent, not a remedy.
3. **Fill in every blank before sending.** An unfilled Annexure B (targets) makes
   §4 unmeasurable, and an unfilled Annexure C makes §2 unenforceable in the
   signatory's favour — which is the half that protects them.

# Part A — The engagement

## 1. Nature of engagement

**1.1** Where the engagement type is **Trial**, this is a structured trial
evaluation period and not a contract of employment. The Participant is engaged
on a non-salaried basis for the duration stated in the Engagement Record,
ordinarily 14 (fourteen) calendar days.

**1.2** A Trial or Internship does not create an employer–employee
relationship, and the Participant is not entitled to statutory employment
benefits for its duration. **Employment** engagements do create that
relationship and carry the entitlements the law attaches to it.

**1.3** The purpose of a Trial is to assess the Participant's suitability,
performance and professional capability for a potential full-time role.

**1.4 Internship.** An Internship is a fixed-term, learning-first engagement of
the duration stated in the Engagement Record. The Company undertakes to give
the Intern real work, a named supervisor, and a written assessment at the end.
The Intern undertakes to treat it as work rather than observation. An
internship carries no expectation of an offer, and none is implied by good
performance.

**1.5 Employment.** An Employment engagement is continuing and salaried, subject
to §22 (probation) and §8.4 (notice). The Participant's role, hours, location
and reporting line are those in the Engagement Record; the Company may vary
them reasonably, in writing, without re-executing this document.

**1.6 Contract.** A Contract engagement is for the defined scope and fee in
Annexure C. The Participant is an independent contractor, is responsible for
their own tax and statutory contributions, and is not held out as an employee.
Nothing in this document creates a partnership or agency beyond the authority
expressly given in writing.

**1.7** The engagement type may be changed only in writing, signed by both
parties, with a fresh Annexure C. Time already served counts toward probation
under §22 when a Trial or Internship converts to Employment.

**1.8** The Participant confirms that everything stated in their application,
CV and interviews is true, and that no material fact has been withheld.
Misrepresentation discovered at any time is a ground for termination under §8.5.

## 2. Compensation & remuneration

**2.1** On a **Trial**, the Participant acknowledges that no fixed salary or
monetary compensation is payable for the Trial Period.

**2.2** On completion of a Trial or Internship the Company will evaluate
performance and may, at its sole discretion:

- **(a)** offer a formal salaried position at the rate mutually agreed and
  recorded in Annexure C; **or**
- **(b)** issue a Certificate of Participation acknowledging the engagement and
  the skills demonstrated.

**2.3** The Participant understands that the issuance of a Certificate does not
constitute a right to employment or to future compensation.

**2.4** Where an offer is extended under 2.2(a), the agreed compensation is that
recorded in Annexure C. That figure binds both parties and may not be
renegotiated without written consent from Tauqeer Mustafa Inc.

**2.5 Internship stipend.** Any stipend is stated in Annexure C. If Annexure C
states none, the internship is unpaid and the Intern has agreed to that in
writing at §26. The Company still bears any cost it asks the Intern to incur
(§18).

**2.6 Salary.** For Employment engagements, salary is stated in Annexure C and
paid monthly, in arrears, by the last working day of each month, by bank
transfer to the account the Participant nominates. Salary is reviewed at the
cadence stated in §22.4; a review is not a promise of an increase.

**2.7 Deductions.** The Company deducts only what law requires or what the
Participant has authorised in writing. Nothing else is deducted from
compensation already earned — not equipment, not training, not shortfall
against a target.

**2.8 Contract fees.** For Contract engagements, fees, milestones and invoicing
terms are in Annexure C. Invoices are settled within 15 (fifteen) days of a
correct invoice against an accepted milestone.

**2.9** Compensation is confidential between the Participant and the Company.
Discussing one's own pay with a regulator, lawyer, or the Company's HR is never
a breach of this clause.

## 3. Duties & responsibilities

Clauses 3.1–3.3 describe the Business Development (B2B) role. For any other
role, Annexure A replaces them and 3.4–3.10 apply unchanged.

**3.1** Conduct outreach (email, LinkedIn, phone) to target SME prospects as
directed by the Company.

**3.2** Manage and update the provided pipeline in the Company portal daily with
outreach activity, responses received and discovery calls booked.

**3.3** Pitch and present the Company's services — cybersecurity consulting,
financial compliance, and SEO/AdSense management — to prospective clients.

**3.4** Participate in daily check-ins and submit the required activity report on
time, every working day.

**3.5** Adhere strictly to all outreach scripts, messaging guidelines and service
positioning materials provided by the Company.

**3.6** Not contact any prospect outside of approved channels, and not represent
the Company without authorisation.

**3.7 Role schedule.** Annexure A states the duties, deliverables and standards
for the Participant's actual role. Where Annexure A and 3.1–3.3 conflict,
Annexure A governs.

**3.8 Reasonable instruction.** Follow the lawful and reasonable instructions of
the reporting manager named in the Engagement Record. An instruction that would
breach this document, the law, or a client's confidence is not reasonable, and
refusing it is protected — raise it under §24.1 instead of carrying it out.

**3.9 Accuracy of records.** Every entry the Participant makes in the portal —
leads, activities, attendance, timesheets, reports, expenses — must be true when
made. Fabricating an activity, a lead, a call, or a check-in is treated as
dishonesty under §8.5, not as a performance issue.

**3.10 Availability.** Be reachable on the approved channels during the working
hours in §11 and respond to internal messages within one working day. Planned
unavailability goes through §11.4.

## 4. Performance evaluation criteria

Performance is evaluated on the following, against the numbers in Annexure B.

**4.1 Primary metric.** The single measure the engagement turns on, named in
Annexure B. For Business Development (B2B) engagements it is the **number of
qualified discovery calls booked with SME prospects**.

**4.2 Pipeline quality.** Completeness of entries, follow-up cadence, and
prospect engagement levels.

**4.3 Daily activity volume.** Outreach sent, responses handled, and the portal
kept current.

**4.4 Professionalism** and communication standards maintained in outreach and
in internal reporting.

**4.5 Cadence.** Trials and Internships are reviewed at the midpoint and at the
end. Employment engagements are reviewed at the end of probation (§22) and at
least every six months after. Every review is written and shared with the
Participant.

**4.6 Targets.** Annexure B states the target for each metric and the date it is
measured on. A target not written into Annexure B before the start date cannot
be used against the Participant.

**4.7 Shortfall.** Missing a target is handled first as support — a written note
of what is short, what changes, and by when. Repeated shortfall after that
becomes a §24.3 performance process, and for a Trial it is simply the outcome
under §2.2(b).

**4.8 The portal is the record.** Evaluation is drawn from the Company portal,
which the Participant can see at all times. There is no separate scorecard the
Participant has not been shown.

# Part B — Protecting the Company and its clients

## 5. Confidentiality & data protection

**5.1** The Participant agrees to keep strictly confidential all business
information, client data, prospect lists, outreach strategies, pricing
structures, service details, internal tools, credentials, communications, and
any proprietary or sensitive information disclosed by the Company — whether
shared verbally, in writing, digitally, or by any other means.

**5.2** The Participant shall not disclose, share, forward, publish, post online,
discuss publicly, or use any confidential information for any purpose other than
performing duties directly under this Agreement.

**5.3** The Participant is strictly prohibited from:

- **(a)** copying, screenshotting, recording or reproducing any Company data,
  documents, tools or client information;
- **(b)** storing Company data on personal devices, personal cloud accounts, or
  any unauthorised platform;
- **(c)** sharing any Company information with family members, friends, or any
  third party under any circumstance;
- **(d)** using any Company data, leads or client information for personal
  freelance work, side business, or competing activities;
- **(e)** forwarding Company emails, files or documents to any external address
  without explicit written authorisation.

**5.4** Any deliberate or negligent data leakage constitutes a critical breach of
this Agreement and may result in immediate termination, legal action, and
financial liability for damage caused to Tauqeer Mustafa Inc.

**5.5** This confidentiality obligation survives the termination or expiry of this
Agreement and remains binding indefinitely, with no expiry date.

**5.6 Personal data.** Prospect, client and colleague records contain personal
data. The Participant will use it only for the task at hand, will not enrich it
from unauthorised sources, will not sell or trade it, and will honour a request
to stop contacting someone the moment it is received. Suppression is logged in
the portal, not remembered.

**5.7 Report a breach within 24 hours.** A lost device, a shared password, a
message sent to the wrong recipient, a file uploaded somewhere it should not be
— report it to the reporting manager and HR within 24 hours of noticing. **A
breach reported promptly and honestly is a mistake and will be handled as one.**
A breach concealed is misconduct under §8.5. This is the most important sentence
in this document.

**5.8 What is not confidential.** Information already public through no fault of
the Participant, information they held before the engagement and can show they
held, and anything a court or regulator compels them to disclose. Nothing here
prevents lawful whistleblowing or cooperating with an authority.

## 6. Intellectual property, copyright & ownership

**6.1** All leads, prospect lists, pipeline data, client communications, outreach
scripts, templates, reports, presentations, strategies, code, designs, and any
other work product generated by the Participant — individually or
collaboratively — during the engagement or any subsequent engagement are the
exclusive intellectual property of Tauqeer Mustafa Inc.

**6.2** All materials, content, documents, scripts and tools provided to or
created by the Participant under this Agreement are protected under the
copyright of Tauqeer Mustafa Inc. The Participant may not claim authorship,
co-authorship, or any ownership interest in any such materials.

**6.3** The Participant is expressly prohibited from:

- **(a)** reproducing, replicating, reselling or distributing any copyrighted
  Company materials in whole or in part;
- **(b)** using any Company scripts, templates, frameworks or strategies for
  personal freelance work or with any other employer;
- **(c)** publishing, uploading or sharing any Company-owned content on personal
  portfolios, social media or public platforms without prior written
  authorisation;
- **(d)** reverse-engineering, adapting or modifying copyrighted Company
  materials for any use outside of this Agreement.

**6.4** The Participant waives any and all claims to ownership, moral rights, or
financial interest in any work product, data or materials produced during the
engagement.

**6.5** On termination or conclusion of this Agreement, the Participant must
immediately delete, destroy or return all Company materials in their possession.
No copies may be retained in any format.

**6.6** Violation of this Section constitutes copyright infringement and a
material breach of contract, for which Tauqeer Mustafa Inc. reserves the right
to pursue all available legal remedies.

**6.7 Present assignment.** To the extent any right does not vest in the Company
automatically, the Participant assigns it to the Company now, for the full term
of protection worldwide, and will sign whatever is reasonably needed to record
that — during the engagement and after it.

**6.8 Pre-existing work.** Anything the Participant made before the engagement,
or outside it and unconnected to it, stays theirs. If they choose to bring it in,
they list it in Annexure A and grant the Company a perpetual, royalty-free
licence to keep using it in whatever it was built into. Without that listing,
work delivered under this engagement is treated as 6.1 work product.

**6.9 Third-party and open-source material.** Do not put anything into Company
work that the Company is not licensed to use. Open-source components are allowed
where the licence permits commercial use; record the component and its licence
in the work itself. Never paste licensed or client-owned material into a
deliverable for another client.

**6.10 Portfolio.** The Participant may state where they worked, their role, and
their results in general terms. They may not reproduce Company or client
material, name a client without written consent, or publish anything covered by
§5 — see also §9.7.

## 7. Non-solicitation & non-poaching

Read this section together with the second drafting note at the top of this
document: §7.1 is a deterrent backed by §5 and §6, not a restraint the Company
should expect a court to enforce on its own terms.

**7.1** During the engagement and for 12 (twelve) months after it concludes, the
Participant agrees not to directly or indirectly solicit, approach or engage any
prospect, lead or client **identified or contacted during the engagement** for
personal or third-party commercial gain, where doing so uses or relies on
confidential information belonging to the Company.

**7.2** Violation of this clause constitutes a material breach of this Agreement.

**7.3 Colleagues.** During the engagement and for 6 (six) months after, the
Participant will not induce a colleague or contractor of the Company to leave in
order to join a competing venture the Participant is part of. Responding to a
public job advertisement is not inducement.

**7.4 Reasonableness.** Nothing in §7 prevents the Participant from working in
the same industry, in the same role, for anyone they choose, or from approaching
a person or company they had a relationship with before this engagement began.
This section restrains the misuse of the Company's confidential relationships,
not the Participant's livelihood. If a court finds any part of §7 wider than the
law permits, §10.6 applies and the remainder stands.

## 8. Termination

**8.1** The Company may terminate a **Trial or Internship** at any time, without
notice or liability, if:

- **(a)** the Participant fails to meet minimum daily activity requirements;
- **(b)** the Participant breaches any term of this Agreement;
- **(c)** the Participant's conduct is found to be unprofessional or harmful to
  the Company's reputation.

**8.2** The Participant may withdraw from a Trial or Internship at any time by
written notice to the Company.

**8.3** On termination or withdrawal, all Company materials must be immediately
returned or deleted. The confidentiality (§5), intellectual property (§6) and
non-solicitation (§7) obligations remain in force.

**8.4 Notice on Employment.** Either party may end an Employment engagement by
written notice: **7 days during probation**, **30 days after confirmation**,
unless Annexure C states otherwise or the law requires longer. The Company may
pay salary in lieu of notice. A Contract engagement ends on completion of scope,
or on 15 days' written notice by either party, with fees due for work accepted to
that date.

**8.5 Summary termination.** The Company may end any engagement immediately,
without notice or payment in lieu, for: dishonesty or fabrication of records
(§3.9); a deliberate or concealed data breach (§5.4, §5.7); theft or fraud
(§17); harassment or violence (§9.4); misrepresentation of the Company to a
client (§9.3); unauthorised competing work using Company assets (§16); or any
conduct that would ordinarily justify dismissal without notice.

**8.6 Exit obligations.** On the last day, the Participant will: hand over every
live lead, client thread and piece of work in the portal with a written handover
note; return all equipment, access cards and documents; confirm in writing that
no Company data remains on any personal device or account (§6.5); and cooperate
with the disabling of their accounts under §12.6.

**8.7 Final settlement.** The Company will settle everything owed — salary or
fees to the last day, approved expenses, and accrued unused leave where §11.4
provides for it — within 30 days of the last working day, against completion of
§8.6.

**8.8** Termination does not extinguish any right or liability that arose before
it, and §5, §6, §7, §17, §19.4 and §10 survive it.

## 9. Conduct & brand representation

**9.1** The Participant agrees to represent the Company professionally and
ethically at all times, in client outreach and in internal interactions alike.

**9.2** The Participant shall not make any representation, promise, guarantee or
commitment to a prospect or client beyond the scope of service offerings
explicitly approved by the Company. When asked something outside that scope, the
answer is *"I will confirm and come back to you."*

**9.3** Any misrepresentation of the Company's services, credentials or pricing is
grounds for immediate termination and may result in further action.

**9.4 Harassment and violence.** The Company has no tolerance for harassment,
sexual harassment, bullying, intimidation, discrimination or violence — toward a
colleague, a client, a prospect, or anyone else encountered through the work, in
person or on any channel. A complaint is handled under §24.2, and the Company
will not retaliate against anyone who raises one in good faith. The Company's
obligations under the Protection Against Harassment of Women at the Workplace
Act 2010 apply in full and are not limited by anything in this document.

**9.5 Equal opportunity.** Hiring, evaluation, pay and progression are decided on
the work. Religion, sect, gender, ethnicity, province of origin, language,
disability, marital status, age and political affiliation are irrelevant to all
four, and treating them as relevant is misconduct.

**9.6 Fitness for work.** Do not work while impaired. Do not bring illegal
substances to any Company premises or client site.

**9.7 Public communication and social media.** Only people authorised in writing
speak for the Company. Otherwise: make it clear that views are personal, never
disclose client names, client work, internal numbers, screenshots of the portal,
or anything under §5, and do not criticise a client publicly. This restricts
speaking *as* or *about* the Company, not the Participant's private life.

**9.8 Client property and premises.** On a client site, follow their rules as if
they were the Company's. Access only the systems and data the engagement
requires — and only what it requires. Curiosity is not authorisation.

**10.2** Any dispute arising from this Agreement shall first be raised directly

**10.6 Severability.** If any provision is held void, unenforceable or wider than

**10.7 No waiver.** A right not exercised is not a right given up. A concession

**10.10 Notices.** In writing, to the Company at the HR address on the signature

**10.11 Counterparts and signatures.** This Agreement may be signed in

and a signed PDF returned by email is a valid original.

**10.12 Language.** Drafted in English. A translation may be provided for
comprehension; the English text governs.

# Part C — Working with us

## 11. Working hours, attendance & leave

**11.1 Hours.** The weekly hours and work location in the Engagement Record.
Where none are stated, the standard week is Monday to Friday, 09:00–18:00
Pakistan Standard Time, with one hour unpaid for lunch.

**11.2 Attendance.** Check in on the portal when the working day starts and check
out when it ends — **Attendance → Check in / Check out**. The check-in is the
attendance record; a message to a manager is not. Checking in for someone else,
or checking in while not working, is falsification under §3.9.

**11.3 Overtime.** No one is expected to work beyond their stated hours as a
matter of routine. Where the Company asks for extra hours, it is agreed in
advance and either paid or given back as time off.

**11.4 Leave.** Requested through **Leave → Request leave**, in advance where the
reason allows, and approved before it is taken. Sick leave is reported on the
first morning and requested retrospectively the same day.

- **Employment:** annual, casual and sick leave as stated in Annexure C, and in
  no case less than the minimum the applicable provincial law provides. Unused
  annual leave is settled under §8.7 to the extent Annexure C provides.
- **Internship / Trial:** no accrued leave entitlement, but reasonable time off
  for illness, an examination or a family emergency will not be refused, and
  will not count against §4 if requested under this clause.
- **Public holidays** as notified by the Government of Pakistan and published in
  the portal.

**11.5 Absence without leave.** Two consecutive working days with no check-in and
no approved leave, and no contact, is treated as absence without leave and is
handled under §24.3.

**11.6 Religious observance.** Prayer breaks, Ramadan hours and Eid holidays are
accommodated. Ask; do not silently disappear.

## 12. Company systems, accounts & credentials

**12.1 What is issued.** A Company email address, a Company mailbox, portal
credentials, a staff number in the `006001` series, and access to the modules the
role requires. All of it belongs to the Company.

**12.2 First sign-in.** The temporary password mailed to the Participant's
personal address is single-use in spirit: change it from **Settings** on first
sign-in, before doing anything else.

**12.3 Passwords.** Unique to the Company account and used nowhere else. Never
reused from a personal account, never shared with anyone including a manager,
never written down where another person can read it, never sent over chat or
email. Enable second-factor authentication wherever the system offers it.

**12.4 No shared or borrowed accounts.** Everything done under an account is
attributed to the person it belongs to. Do not sign in as a colleague, do not let
a colleague sign in as you, and do not create an account for a person who has
not been onboarded through HR.

**12.5 Company email.** The Company mailbox is for Company business. Do not use
it to register personal services, and do not use a personal address to conduct
Company business — a client thread that lives in a personal inbox is a §5.3(e)
breach and is invisible to the handover in §8.6.

**12.6 Access ends when the engagement ends.** On the last working day the
Company disables the account, revokes the credentials, and retains the mailbox
contents as a business record. The Participant has no right of access to it
afterwards and will not attempt access. Anything personal must be removed before
that day.

**12.7 Least privilege.** Access is granted for the role, not for the person. Do
not ask a colleague to fetch data your own access does not cover, and tell HR if
your access is wider than your role needs.

## 13. Acceptable use & information security

**13.1 The portal is where company data lives.** Every lead, client record,
activity, document and report goes into the Company portal as it is created — not
into a personal spreadsheet, not a notes app, not a screenshot, not a WhatsApp
thread, not a personal cloud drive. This is the operative form of §5.3(b) and it
is the rule most often broken by accident.

**13.2 Prohibited.** Installing unapproved software on Company equipment;
disabling antivirus, updates, or disk encryption; connecting Company equipment to
an untrusted network without a VPN; plugging in unknown removable media; using
public Wi-Fi for client data without a VPN; sharing a Company file by public link.

**13.3 Phishing.** Verify before acting on any message that asks for credentials,
a payment, a change of bank details, or urgency. A request from a "client" or a
"director" to move money or send data is confirmed by a second channel before it
is acted on, no matter who it appears to be from. Reporting a suspicious message
is always correct, even if it turns out to be genuine.

**13.4 Encryption and screens.** Full-disk encryption on any device holding
Company data. Lock the screen when stepping away. Do not display client data in a
public place where it can be read over a shoulder.

**13.5 The Information Security Policy.** The Company's Information Security
Policy, as issued and updated, forms part of these terms. Where it is stricter
than this section, it governs.

**13.6 Security is not a specialist's job.** The Company sells cybersecurity
consulting. A member of this Company leaking client data does more damage to it
than to almost any other business, and the Participant accepts that this raises
the standard expected of them rather than lowering it.

## 14. Equipment & personal devices

**14.1 Company equipment** is issued for Company work, listed in Annexure A on
issue, kept in good condition, insured by the Company against ordinary risk, and
returned on the last working day under §8.6. Reasonable wear is expected and is
never charged back (§2.7).

**14.2 Loss or damage** is reported the same day. Loss through negligence may be
recovered only where the Participant has agreed to it in writing after the event;
nothing is deducted unilaterally.

**14.3 Personal devices.** Where the Participant works from their own device, the
device must have a screen lock, current updates, disk encryption and no
jailbreak; Company data is accessed only through the portal and the Company
mailbox, and never downloaded to local storage or synced into a personal cloud
account. The Company may require a device to be brought up to this standard, or
may withdraw access from it.

**14.4 Personal use** of Company equipment is tolerated within reason. It carries
no expectation of privacy on that equipment — see §19.

**14.5 No repurposing.** Company equipment is not used for other paid work, for a
side business, or by anyone who is not the Participant.

## 15. Artificial intelligence & third-party tools

**15.1 Approved tools only.** Use the tools the Company provides or approves. A
new tool — including any AI assistant, browser extension, scraper, automation or
enrichment service — is approved in writing before any Company or client data
touches it.

**15.2 Never paste confidential data into a public AI tool.** Client names,
contact details, credentials, contracts, pricing, code, internal documents,
pipeline exports: none of it goes into a consumer chatbot, a free
document-summarising site, a public translation service, or any tool whose terms
allow training on submitted content. This is a §5.3 breach and is treated as one.

**15.3 AI-assisted work is the Participant's work.** Using an AI tool for drafting
or research where it is approved is fine. The Participant remains responsible for
accuracy, and may not present AI-generated material as verified fact, send it to
a client unreviewed, or use it to fabricate a record under §3.9.

**15.4 No automated outreach outside the approved stack.** Bulk senders,
unapproved scrapers, and account-automation tools are prohibited — they endanger
the Company's sending reputation and can breach the platforms' own terms, which is
outside "approved channels" under §3.6.

**15.5 Anything built with an AI tool** during the engagement is still §6.1 work
product, and any licence attaching to a tool's output is disclosed under §6.9.

## 16. Conflict of interest & outside work

**16.1 Declare it.** Any interest that could conflict with the Company's — a
family member at a client or competitor, a shareholding in one, a personal
relationship with a prospect, a directorship, a role in another business — is
declared to HR in writing on joining and whenever it arises.

**16.2 Outside work.** Interns, Trial Participants and Contractors may hold other
work, provided it does not compete with the Company, does not use Company time,
data, equipment or relationships, and does not create a §16.1 conflict.
Employees seek written approval first; it will not be unreasonably refused.

**16.3 Never on both sides.** Do not take payment, commission, referral fee or
gift from a client, prospect, supplier or competitor in connection with Company
work. Route every commercial arrangement through the Company.

**16.4 Company opportunity.** A business opportunity that comes to the Participant
because of their role belongs to the Company and is brought to the Company, not
taken personally or passed to a third party.

## 17. Anti-bribery, gifts & fraud

**17.1 No bribery.** Do not offer, give, request or accept anything of value to
improperly influence a decision — to win work, to speed up a process, or to
obtain data. This applies to public officials absolutely, with no facilitation
payments.

**17.2 Gifts and hospitality.** Modest, occasional and transparent is acceptable
and is declared to the reporting manager. Cash and cash equivalents are never
acceptable. Anything offered during a live tender or negotiation is declined.

**17.3 Fraud.** Falsifying an invoice, an expense, a timesheet, an activity, a
report, a qualification or a client approval is fraud, is a §8.5 ground, and may
be referred to the authorities.

**17.4 No retaliation.** Anyone who reports a suspected bribe, fraud or conflict
in good faith is protected, even if the suspicion proves unfounded.

## 18. Expenses & company money

**18.1 Pre-approval.** Expenses are approved in advance by the reporting manager,
except where an amount is pre-authorised in Annexure C.

**18.2 Reimbursement.** Submitted with a receipt through the portal within 30 days,
and reimbursed with the next payment run. Where the Company asks the Participant
to incur a cost, the Company bears it — including on an unpaid internship (§2.5).

**18.3 Company funds.** Never held in a personal account, never mixed with
personal money, and never moved on an instruction that has not been verified
under §13.3.

## 19. Monitoring, audit & investigation

**19.1 What is monitored.** Company systems are logged: portal activity, mailbox
send and receive records, attendance check-ins, and access to client data. This
exists to protect client data and to resolve disputes about what happened.

**19.2 Proportionate.** Monitoring is of Company systems, in the ordinary course.
The Company does not read private messages on personal accounts, does not install
keystroke or webcam surveillance on personal devices, and does not track location
outside working hours.

**19.3 Consent.** The Participant consents to §19.1 and acknowledges that Company
equipment and accounts carry no expectation of privacy (§14.4).

**19.4 Investigation.** Where a breach is suspected, the Company may suspend
access, preserve records, and require the Participant's cooperation. Suspension
on full pay pending investigation is not a disciplinary finding. The Participant
is told what is alleged and is heard before any decision under §24.3.

## 20. Health, safety & remote working

**20.1** The Company will provide a safe place and safe systems of work, and will
not require work that is unsafe.

**20.2 Remote working.** The Participant is responsible for a workspace that is
safe to sit and work in, with adequate light, seating and connectivity, and will
tell the Company if it is not.

**20.3 Working time and rest.** Take the breaks in §11.1. Persistent
out-of-hours work is a problem to raise under §24.1, not a virtue.

**20.4 Wellbeing.** If health — physical or mental — is affecting the work, say so
early. It will be treated as a health matter under §11.4 and §24.3, in
confidence, not as a performance failure.

**20.5 Incidents** are reported to the reporting manager and HR the same day.

## 21. Verification & right to work

**21.1** The engagement is conditional on satisfactory verification of identity
(CNIC or passport), the right to work in Pakistan, the qualifications and
employment history relied on, and — where the role requires it and the law
permits — references and a criminal-record check.

**21.2** The Participant will provide accurate documents on request and consents
to their verification. A document later found to be false is a §8.5 ground.

**21.3 Data held about the Participant.** The Company keeps personnel records for
as long as the law and its legitimate interests require, protects them under §13,
and will correct an error on request.

**21.4 Minimum age.** No engagement is offered to anyone below the minimum age
permitted by the applicable provincial law.

## 22. Probation, confirmation & progression

*Employment engagements only.*

**22.1 Probation** is 3 (three) months from the start date, unless Annexure C
states otherwise. Time already served on a Trial or Internship in the same role
counts toward it (§1.7).

**22.2 Extension.** Probation may be extended once, by up to 3 months, in writing,
with the reasons and what must change stated.

**22.3 Confirmation** is written. If the Company neither confirms nor extends by
the end of probation, the Participant is confirmed.

**22.4 Review and progression.** Performance and salary are reviewed at
confirmation and at least every 6 months after. Progression is decided on §4
criteria and on Annexure B outcomes. A review is not a promise of an increase
(§2.6).

## 23. Certificates, references & alumni

**23.1 Certificate.** On completion of an Internship or Trial, or of at least 3
months of Employment, the Company will issue a certificate stating the role,
the dates and the work done. It is issued within 15 days of the last working day
and is not withheld as leverage.

**23.2 Reference.** The Company will confirm role and dates to a future employer
on request. Any evaluative reference is honest and given in good faith.

**23.3 No certificate for a §8.5 exit,** and none where §8.6 has not been
completed.

**23.4 Alumni.** §5, §6 and §7 continue to apply after the engagement. Otherwise
the Company parts on good terms and is glad to be named as past experience within
§6.10.

## 24. Grievances & discipline

**24.1 Raise it.** Any concern — about the work, a decision, a colleague,
workload, safety, or this document — goes first to the reporting manager, and to
HR if the manager is the subject of it or does not resolve it. HR responds within
7 working days.

**24.2 Harassment complaints** go directly to HR or to the Company's designated
inquiry committee, may bypass the reporting manager entirely, and are handled
under §9.4 and the Protection Against Harassment of Women at the Workplace Act
2010. Confidentiality is maintained so far as the investigation allows.

**24.3 Discipline.** Except for a §8.5 ground, the Company follows a fair
sequence: the concern is put in writing; the Participant is given the evidence
and a real opportunity to respond; the outcome and the reasons are given in
writing. Ordinary steps are an informal conversation, a written warning, a final
written warning, then termination on notice.

**24.4 Appeal.** Any disciplinary outcome may be appealed in writing within 7
days, to someone who did not make the original decision.

**24.5 No retaliation** for using this section in good faith.

## 25. Force majeure & business continuity

**25.1** Neither party is in breach for a failure caused by something genuinely
outside its control — natural disaster, war, epidemic, a nationwide internet or
power failure, or an act of government.

**25.2** The affected party tells the other promptly and both act reasonably to
work around it, including working remotely where that is possible.

**25.3** Force majeure suspends performance; it does not cancel amounts already
earned. Where it persists beyond 30 days, either party may end the engagement
under §8 with settlement under §8.7.
